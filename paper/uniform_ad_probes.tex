\begin{abstract}
\normalsize
We construct a single trace invariant that separates all connected trees of order at most $N$. The invariant is the trace of an explicit self-adjoint polynomial $p_N\in\Z\langle x,y\rangle$, where $x,y$ are noncommuting variables evaluated at the adjacency and degree matrices. Its arithmetic circuit has $O(N^2)$ gates, formal degree at most $3N-2$, and polynomial bit complexity. The circuit is determined by $N$ alone and operates on matrices of the original input order. A linear lower bound shows that the trace degree has the correct asymptotic scale.

The main ingredient is a fixed spanning family for the joint cyclic module of a real symmetric matrix pair from any prescribed vector. In dimension at most $N$, when the second matrix has spectral diameter at most $N-1$, a triangular recurrence gives $N(N+1)/2$ integer noncommutative polynomials of degree at most $N-1$, computed by a shared $O(N^2)$ circuit. This replaces the exponentially large family of all short words without increasing the spanning degree. The proof uses a rational differential connection with a small self-adjoint residue: a primitive polynomial exterior vector representing an invariant subspace must have an integer degree smaller than one. For trees, pointed adjacency--degree reconstruction, McKay's distance polynomials, and a factored integer encoding turn this spanning family into the single trace invariant.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
How much spectral information is needed to determine a tree? The adjacency spectrum alone is insufficient: almost all trees have a nonisomorphic cospectral mate \cite{Schwenk}. Even the spectra of $A_T-tD_T$ for every real $t$ can agree for nonisomorphic trees \cite{GodsilSun,FanEtAl}. McKay showed that the spectra of all adjacency--degree polynomials determine a tree \cite[Theorem 5.3]{McKay}. We ask for a quantitative form of this determination: for each order bound $N$, can one prescribe a single adjacency--degree polynomial whose trace distinguishes all trees of order at most $N$, with low degree, a small arithmetic circuit, and polynomial bit complexity? Such an expression evaluates by the same sequence of matrix operations on every input and respects vertex relabelling through simultaneous conjugation. Controlling all three resources makes the passage from complete spectral data to a finite algebraic invariant explicit.

McKay's algebraic pruning procedure reconstructs the tree by successive trace tests \cite[Algorithm 5.4]{McKay}. These tests select rooted types and the multiplicities of adjacent leaves, and the procedure updates the residual adjacency and degree operators. Its choices therefore depend on the input trace data. The procedure by itself does not specify a single circuit fixed by $N$ with the simultaneous resource bounds sought here. Our construction uses a fixed family of polynomials to determine a representation before applying tree reconstruction; the spanning argument applies to general symmetric matrix pairs and uses no tree pruning.

The pointed reconstruction theorem of Lu and Li continues this spectral approach: the moments $\one^{\mathsf T}w(A_T,D_T)\one$, over all words $w$ in the adjacency matrix $A_T$ and degree matrix $D_T$, already determine the tree \cite[Theorem 5.4]{LuLi}. The present work makes this description uniformly finite with explicit arithmetic resources. The vectors $w(A_T,D_T)\one$ span its principal module, of dimension at most $N$. The responses of words of length at most $N-1$ already span this module, but there are $2^N-1$ such words. Selecting a basis after evaluation reduces their number while making the selection depend on the tree \cite[Proposition 6.1 and Remark 6.2]{LuLi}. We replace this exponential word family by a fixed quadratic-size polynomial spanning family, preserving the spanning degree and obtaining the following resource bounds.

Throughout, $\Z\langle x,y\rangle$ is the free associative algebra in the two \emph{noncommuting} variables $x,y$. The involution $p\mapsto p^*$ fixes $x,y$ and reverses each word. Our circuits have the identity and the two input matrices as inputs, and use addition, ordinary matrix multiplication, and multiplication by prescribed integer constants. The involution is compiled by reversing products. Circuit size counts stored instructions, including inputs.

\begin{theorem}[A uniform linear-degree tree invariant]\label{thm:tree}
For every $N\ge1$, a deterministic algorithm running in time polynomial in $N$ constructs a self-adjoint polynomial $p_N\in\Z\langle x,y\rangle$, represented by an arithmetic circuit, such that
\[
T\cong T'
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\tr p_N(A_T,D_T)=\tr p_N(A_{T'},D_{T'})
\]
for all connected trees $T,T'$ of orders at most $N$. The circuit has $O(N^2)$ gates and formal degree at most $3N-2$. Its scalar constants have $O(N\log(N+1))$ bits. For every such tree, the bit length of each stored matrix entry and of the final trace is $O(N^5\log(N+1))$.
\end{theorem}

Every gate operates on matrices of the original input order using the operations specified above. The circuit and its constants are determined by $N$ alone, and the only scalar readout is the trace of its final output. The simultaneous resource bounds matter. The same reconstruction and integer encoding applied to all $2^N-1$ short words already gives a single trace polynomial of degree at most $3N-2$: two words contribute at most $2N-2$, an inserted generator at most one, and McKay's expression for $\one\one^{\mathsf T}$ at most $N-1$. The direct encoding of the resulting Gram data, however, has exponentially many digit positions and exponential bit length. The smaller spanning family reduces the number of encoded entries to $O(N^4)$, giving polynomial bit length. Factoring their weights then gives the $O(N^2)$ gate bound.

Tree canonization itself admits logarithmic-space algorithms \cite{Lindell}, including a construction of a polynomial canon \cite{ADFK}. Other degree-based spectral refinements use multivariate spectra with auxiliary rank-one matrices built from degree-class indicators \cite{WangZhao}. Our question concerns the resources of a fixed expression in the adjacency and degree matrices alone. Nonisomorphic spiders give a trace-degree lower bound of $2\lfloor(N-3)/3\rfloor$ for $N\ge9$ (Section~\ref{sec:lower}), so our invariant has the correct asymptotic degree scale.

The spanning construction applies to general real symmetric pairs. It provides a fixed finite description of the joint cyclic module from any prescribed vector, including when that module is a proper subspace. Together with Gram data, this gives explicit finite invariants for pointed self-adjoint representations (Corollary~\ref{cor:pointed}), so the algebraic construction extends beyond tree reconstruction. For $H,K\in M_n(\R)$ and $v\in\R^n$, define
\[
\M(H,K;v)=\spanop_{\R}\{w(H,K)v:w\in\{x,y\}^{*}\},
\]
including the empty word. For a tree, write $\M_T=\M(A_T,D_T;\one)$ for its principal module. Fix $N\ge1$ and define $U_{i,j}\in\Z\langle x,y\rangle$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:triangle}
U_{0,0}=1,\qquad
U_{i+1,j}=\bigl(y+N^2(j-i)\bigr)U_{i,j}
                 +N^2xU_{i,j-1},
\end{equation}
with $U_{i,j}=0$ whenever $j\notin\{0,\ldots,i\}$.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform spanning probes]\label{thm:probes}
Let $1\le n\le N$, let $H,K\in M_n(\R)$ be symmetric, and suppose
\[
\lambda_{\max}(K)-\lambda_{\min}(K)\le N-1.
\]
Then, for every $v\in\R^n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:span}
\spanop_{\R}\{U_{i,j}(H,K)v:0\le j\le i<N\}=\M(H,K;v).
\end{equation}
The family in \eqref{eq:triangle} is constructed from $N$ alone. It has $N(N+1)/2$ members, shared circuit size $O(N^2)$, and formal degree at most $N-1$. Its scalar constants have $O(\log(N+1))$ bits.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Rank-sensitive prefix]\label{cor:rankprefix}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:probes}, if $r=\dim\M(H,K;v)$, then
\[
\spanop_{\R}\{U_{i,j}(H,K)v:0\le j\le i<r\}
=\M(H,K;v).
\]
For $r=0$ this is the empty span. The construction evaluates the full family indexed by $0\le j\le i<N$ and does not need to determine $r$.
\end{corollary}

Either generator may play the bounded role; the ordered statement places it in the second slot. Interchanging the generators gives the corresponding spanning statement for $U_{i,j}(K,H)v$ when $\diam\Spec(H)\le N-1$. For trees, we use $H=A_T$, $K=D_T$. The family may be redundant, and the same formulas apply to arbitrary seeds and proper cyclic modules. Its degree $N-1$ is necessary in the worst case (Proposition~\ref{prop:probedegree}). For example, the mixed probes include
\[
\begin{aligned}
U_{2,1}&=N^2(yx+xy),\\
U_{3,1}&=N^2\bigl(y^2x+yxy+xy^2-N^2yx-2N^2xy\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]
The lower-degree coefficients depend on multiplication order. The circuit bound concerns the shared recurrence; its monomial expansion need not have polynomial size.

For the proof, set $B=K/N^2$ and introduce an auxiliary variable $t$. The differential Krylov sequence $q_0=v$, $q_{i+1}=\nabla q_i$, for $\nabla=\partial_t+B/t+H$, spans an invariant rational subspace. Self-adjointness bounds the degree of a primitive polynomial exterior vector representing this subspace by $N\,\diam\Spec(B)<1$. This degree is a nonnegative integer, so the exterior vector is constant. The subspace is therefore constant, and connection invariance makes its constant part invariant under both $H$ and $K$. It contains $v$, and every iterate already lies in the rational extension of the joint cyclic module; hence the two constant spaces coincide. Taking coefficients of $N^{2i}t^iq_i$ now gives \eqref{eq:triangle}. Only the residue needs a spectral-diameter bound; symmetry of $H$ supplies the inner-product cancellation. Section~\ref{sec:spanning} compares this argument with degree-integrality arguments for small-exponent differential systems \cite{GV,BG}.

The same self-adjoint argument extends to several symmetric generators.
For symmetric $H,A_1,\ldots,A_s\in M_n(\R)$ with $s\ge1$, $1\le n\le N$, and
$\diam\Spec(A_i)\le N-1$, a fixed family of
$N+sN(N-1)/2$ integer noncommutative polynomial probes spans the joint cyclic module
from every seed, with the same degree bound $N-1$ and rank-sensitive prefix.
A shared circuit uses $O(s^2N^2)$ gates at the original matrix order.
There is still no spectral bound on $H$. We outline the extension after
the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:probes}; Appendix~\ref{sec:multi} gives its
recurrence and proof.


\begin{remark}[Fixed words versus polynomial probes]\label{rem:wordlower}
For $N\ge5$, any fixed family of words of length at most $N-1$ whose responses span $\M(H,K;v)$ for every input in Theorem~\ref{thm:probes} must contain at least $2^{N-1}$ words. To see this, colour the edges of an $N$-vertex path arbitrarily with $x,y$, let $H,K$ be the adjacency matrices of the two colour classes, and take $v=e_1$ at one endpoint. The matrix $K$ is a disjoint union of path adjacency matrices, so $\diam\Spec(K)<4\le N-1$. Among all words of length at most $N-1$, exactly one has a nonzero $e_N$ coordinate when applied to $e_1$: the word following the edge colours from one endpoint to the other, read in the order of matrix action. Its $e_N$ coordinate is one. Thus every spanning word family must contain that word. Varying the colouring forces all $2^{N-1}$ words of length $N-1$. In the general symmetric-pair setting, Theorem~\ref{thm:probes} therefore gives an exponential reduction from fixed words to polynomial probes at the same optimal degree.
\end{remark}

To obtain the tree invariant, enumerate the family as $U_0,\ldots,U_{M-1}$ and put $z_i=U_i(H,K)v$. The three matrices
\[
G_{ij}=z_i^{\mathsf T}z_j,\qquad
G^H_{ij}=z_i^{\mathsf T}Hz_j,\qquad
G^K_{ij}=z_i^{\mathsf T}Kz_j
\]
determine the pointed representation: $G$ determines linear relations and inner products, and $G^H,G^K$ determine the actions of $H,K$. Hence they determine every pointed moment. For trees, use $(H,K,v)=(A_T,D_T,\one)$ and apply pointed reconstruction. McKay's distance polynomials express $\one\one^{\mathsf T}$ as a polynomial of degree at most $N-1$ \cite[Lemma 5.1 and Corollary 5.2]{McKay}, converting these entries into traces. Bounded integer entries can then be packed into one trace. Factoring this encoding permits $O(N^2)$ gates despite the $O(N^4)$ entries.

\paragraph{Related work.}
Pointed moments also form a series represented by a weighted automaton. Tzeng's equivalence algorithm and later refinements construct input-dependent reachable bases; polynomial identity testing also gives randomized equivalence tests \cite{Tzeng,KMOWW}. Our self-adjoint setting admits fixed exact polynomial probes. Fixed short word families span the full matrix algebra on generic tuples \cite{KlepSpenko,JiaCapel}, whereas our polynomial family works throughout the stated self-adjoint class. Matrix-algebra length bounds control the span of all short words \cite{Paz,Pappacena,Shitov,Sprung}; the full algebra may have dimension $n^2$, while the cyclic module used here has dimension at most $n$.

Trace-generation theorems \cite{Procesi} and trace identities \cite{Razmyslov} provide the classical invariant-theoretic setting. Trace criteria also classify systems of maps between real inner-product spaces \cite[Corollary 1]{FHS}, and finite Gram criteria for unitary similarity were developed by Al'pin and Ikramov \cite[Proposition 10 and Theorem 5]{AlpinIkramov}. Derksen and Makam construct input-dependent pivot-word bases and separating words \cite[Algorithm 4.8 and Section 5]{DM}; Forbes and Shpilka obtain explicit quasipolynomial-size separating families through polynomial identity testing (full version, \cite[Theorem 1.13]{FS}). Our contribution is the fixed low-degree spanning recurrence for self-adjoint pairs and its use to achieve the simultaneous circuit and bit bounds.

\paragraph{Outline.}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:tree} proceeds from a fixed spanning family to complete finite data and then to a single trace circuit. Section~\ref{sec:spanning} establishes the spanning family for general symmetric pairs: differential rigidity identifies the span of the iterates with the joint cyclic module, and coefficient extraction gives the fixed probes. Section~\ref{sec:reconstruction} uses their Gram data to recover the pointed representation and hence every principal moment. Principal-moment reconstruction then makes these finite data a complete tree invariant.

The remaining task is to realize this invariant within the stated circuit model. McKay's distance polynomials turn the Gram entries into traces, and uniform integer bounds allow all entries to be encoded injectively in one trace. Factoring the encoding gives the quadratic gate bound; the degrees of its factors and the number of encoded entries give the degree and bit bounds. The circuit evaluates this factored expression directly. Section~\ref{sec:lower} then shows that linear trace degree is necessary up to a constant factor.

The appendices branch from the spanning theorem. Appendix~\ref{sec:cyclic} uses the probes to construct a single positive semidefinite operator with the prescribed principal module and a cyclic all-ones vector. Appendix~\ref{sec:fulltrace} recovers the full adjacency--degree representation in the Frobenius matrix space, allowing reconstruction directly from McKay's theorem at higher resource cost. Appendix~\ref{sec:verification} records the computational checks and the scope of formal verification. Appendix~\ref{sec:multi} extends the same rigidity argument to several symmetric generators while preserving the degree bound and rank-sensitive prefix.

\section{Uniform spanning via differential rigidity}\label{sec:spanning}
To prove Theorem~\ref{thm:probes}, we must show that the fixed recurrence
\eqref{eq:triangle} spans the entire joint cyclic module on every admissible
input. We first identify the rational span of a differential Krylov sequence
from $v$ with $\M(H,K;v)\otimes_\R\R(t)$. The rigidity lemma below forces
this rational span to be constant and jointly invariant under the two matrices.
Taking coefficients of the iterates then gives the prescribed probes.
Throughout, $t$ is a commuting auxiliary variable; the polynomials $U_{i,j}$
remain elements of $\Z\langle x,y\rangle$.

For a linear map $B$ on $\R^n$, its additive action on the $r$th exterior power is
\[
B^{[r]}(u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge u_r)
=\sum_{j=1}^r u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge Bu_j\wedge\cdots\wedge u_r.
\]
If $B$ is symmetric, then $B^{[r]}$ is symmetric for the induced inner product,
and its eigenvalues are sums of $r$ eigenvalues of $B$ with distinct indices.
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diam}
\diam\Spec(B^{[r]})\le r\,\diam\Spec(B).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Small-residue rigidity]\label{lem:rigidity}
Let $B,H\in M_n(\R)$ be symmetric, and let $V\subseteq\R(t)^n$ be an
$r$-dimensional subspace invariant under the connection
\[
\nabla f=f'+(B/t+H)f.
\]
If $r\,\diam\Spec(B)<1$, then
$V=W\otimes_{\R}\R(t)$ for a real subspace $W\subseteq\R^n$ invariant under
both $B$ and $H$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The cases $r=0$ and $r=n$ are immediate, so assume $0<r<n$.
Take the exterior product of a rational basis of $V$. Clear its denominators
and divide its coordinates by their polynomial gcd to obtain
\[
w(t)=w_0+w_1t+\cdots+w_\ell t^\ell
\in \bigl(\textstyle\bigwedge^r\R^n\bigr)[t].
\]
This vector is nonzero and its coordinates are coprime. In particular,
$w_0\ne0$, since otherwise all coordinates would be divisible by $t$; also
$w_\ell\ne0$ by the choice of $\ell$.

Invariance of $V$ makes the rational line spanned by $w$ invariant under the
induced connection. Hence there is $c(t)\in\R(t)$ such that
\[
tw'+(B^{[r]}+tH^{[r]})w=c(t)w.
\]
The left side is polynomial. A polynomial Bezout identity for the coprime
coordinates of $w$ therefore shows that $c(t)$ is polynomial. Comparing
degrees gives $\deg c\le1$, so write $c(t)=\alpha+\beta t$.
The constant coefficient and the coefficient of $t^{\ell+1}$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endcoeff}
B^{[r]}w_0=\alpha w_0,
\qquad H^{[r]}w_\ell=\beta w_\ell.
\end{equation}
If $\ell>0$, the coefficient of $t^\ell$ gives
\[
\ell w_\ell+B^{[r]}w_\ell+H^{[r]}w_{\ell-1}
=\alpha w_\ell+\beta w_{\ell-1}.
\]
Take the inner product with $w_\ell$. Symmetry of $H^{[r]}$ and
\eqref{eq:endcoeff} cancel the terms containing $w_{\ell-1}$. Since
$\alpha\in\Spec(B^{[r]})$, the Rayleigh quotient of $B^{[r]}$ then yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integergap}
\ell=\alpha-
\frac{\langle w_\ell,B^{[r]}w_\ell\rangle}{\|w_\ell\|^2}
\le\diam\Spec(B^{[r]})
\le r\,\diam\Spec(B)<1.
\end{equation}
This is impossible for the positive integer $\ell$. Thus $\ell=0$.

The constant exterior vector $w_0$ determines a constant subspace: explicitly,
$V$ consists of the rational vectors $u$ satisfying $u\wedge w_0=0$.
This equation has constant real coefficients, so its solution space is
$W\otimes_\R\R(t)$ for a real subspace $W\subseteq\R^n$.
For $u\in W$, invariance gives $(B/t+H)u\in W\otimes_\R\R(t)$.
Multiply by $t$ and pass to $\R^n/W$. The constant and linear coefficients
of $Bu+tHu$ vanish separately, proving $Bu,Hu\in W$.
\end{proof}

The strict bound in the lemma is necessary: for
$B=\operatorname{diag}(0,-1)$ and $H=0$, the nonconstant rational line
spanned by $(1,t)^{\mathsf T}$ is horizontal for $\partial_t+B/t$.
Its primitive polynomial degree and the spectral diameter of $B$ both equal one.

We apply the lemma to the rational span of the differential iterates from $v$.
The goal is to identify this span with $\M(H,K;v)\otimes_\R\R(t)$; the number
of iterates required will then equal $\dim_\R\M(H,K;v)$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:probes} and Corollary~\ref{cor:rankprefix}]
If $v=0$, both sides of the spanning identities are the zero subspace. Assume $v\ne0$,
put $B=K/N^2$, and define
\[
q_0=v,\qquad q_{i+1}=\nabla q_i,
\qquad \nabla=\partial_t+B/t+H.
\]
Let $s\ge1$ be the first index for which $q_s$ belongs to the
$\R(t)$-span of $q_0,\ldots,q_{s-1}$. Such an index exists with $s\le n$.
Write $q_s=\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}a_i(t)q_i$. The Leibniz rule gives
\[
q_{s+1}
=\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}a_i'(t)q_i
 +\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}a_i(t)q_{i+1}.
\]
Every term on the right lies in the same span. More generally, that span is
invariant under $\nabla$, because $\nabla q_i=q_{i+1}$ and differentiating
a rational coefficient preserves $\R(t)$. Thus all later iterates lie in it,
and
\[
V:=\spanop_{\R(t)}\{q_i:i\ge0\}
=\spanop_{\R(t)}\{q_0,\ldots,q_{s-1}\},
\qquad \dim_{\R(t)}V=s.
\]

Let $\M=\M(H,K;v)$. It is invariant under $H$ and $K$, so the recurrence
for $q_i$ gives $V\subseteq\M\otimes_\R\R(t)$. Also,
\[
s\,\diam\Spec(B)\le\frac{N(N-1)}{N^2}<1.
\]
Lemma~\ref{lem:rigidity} therefore gives $V=W\otimes_\R\R(t)$ with
$W$ invariant under $H,K$ and containing $v$. The definition of $\M$ implies
$\M\subseteq W$, and hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diffspan}
V=\M\otimes_\R\R(t).
\end{equation}
In particular, $s=r:=\dim_\R\M$.
This is where the spectral-diameter bound on $K$ enters: $K/N^2$ is the
residue in the connection, while symmetry of $H$ supplies the cancellation
in \eqref{eq:integergap}.

To extract fixed polynomial expressions from these iterates, put $f_i=t^iq_i$.
Their recurrence is
\[
f_0=v,\qquad
f_{i+1}=tf_i'+(B-iI+tH)f_i.
\]
Induction shows that $f_i$ is a polynomial vector of degree at most $i$.
Write
\[
f_i(t)=N^{-2i}\sum_{j=0}^i c_{i,j}t^j.
\]
Comparison of coefficients gives
\[
c_{0,0}=v,\qquad
c_{i+1,j}=\bigl(K+N^2(j-i)I\bigr)c_{i,j}
             +N^2Hc_{i,j-1},
\]
with coefficients outside the indicated range taken to be zero. This is
exactly \eqref{eq:triangle}, so $c_{i,j}=U_{i,j}(H,K)v$.

Let $S$ be the real span of the coefficients of $f_0,\ldots,f_{r-1}$.
The coefficient recurrence gives $S\subseteq\M$. Conversely,
$q_i=t^{-i}f_i\in S\otimes_\R\R(t)$ for $i<r$, and these $q_i$ span $V$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:diffspan} therefore gives
$\M\otimes_\R\R(t)\subseteq S\otimes_\R\R(t)$.
Taking dimensions yields $S=\M$. Consequently,
\[
\spanop_\R\{U_{i,j}(H,K)v:0\le j\le i<r\}=\M.
\]
Since $r\le N$, this proves both spanning assertions. The circuit evaluates
the full family with $0\le j\le i<N$, independently of $r$.

It remains to bound the resources. There are
$\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}(i+1)=N(N+1)/2$ polynomials, and each new entry of
\eqref{eq:triangle} uses a constant number of gates from the preceding
entries. The shared circuit therefore has $O(N^2)$ gates. Induction in
\eqref{eq:triangle} gives $\deg U_{i,j}\le i$, so the formal degree is at most
$N-1$. The prescribed integer scalars have absolute value at most $N^3$,
and thus $O(\log(N+1))$ bits. All choices in this recurrence depend only on $N$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Several symmetric generators.}
The mechanism extends by placing the additional generators at distinct
real poles. For
$\nabla=\partial_t+H+\sum_{i=1}^sB_i/(t-a_i)$, the primitive exterior-vector
argument bounds its degree $\ell$ by
$\ell\le r\sum_i\diam\Spec(B_i)$. Each pole supplies an eigenvalue of
$B_i^{[r]}$, and the highest two coefficients supply a sum of Rayleigh
quotients; the self-adjointness of $H$ gives the same cancellation as above.
Thus $r\sum_i\diam\Spec(B_i)<1$ again forces constancy. Taking
$a_i=i-1$ and $B_i=A_i/(sN^2)$ meets this condition uniformly. Clearing
denominators in the differential iterates then yields a fixed integer
recurrence, whose first $r$ rows already span the joint cyclic module.
The polynomial coefficients in row $k$ still have noncommutative degree
at most $k$. Appendix~\ref{sec:multi} provides the details, including
the shared circuit bound. This extension uses the same self-adjoint
rigidity argument without a block enlargement.

The differential-module framework is standard \cite[Chapter~2]{vdPS}.
Classical cyclic-vector constructions, such as Katz's algorithm \cite{Katz},
select a vector whose differential iterates form a basis. Here the seed is
prescribed: Lemma~\ref{lem:rigidity} identifies its differential cyclic module
with the joint invariant module of the two matrices. The use of degree
integrality also has precedents in constant-reduction arguments.
Gontsov and Vyugin bound a nonpositive subbundle degree strictly above $-1$
and hence force it to vanish \cite[Lemma~2]{GV}.
Barkatou and Gontsov give a Liouvillian-solvability criterion using differences
of the real parts of formal exponents \cite[Theorem~2.3]{BG}. Their criterion
also requires distinct leading eigenvalues at irregular singularities and
exponent differences outside $\Q\setminus\Z$ at Fuchsian singularities.
The latter condition already fails for our horizontal tree system
$f'=-(D_T/(N^2t)+A_T)f$ whenever $T$ has distinct vertex degrees: the residue
then has nonzero rational eigenvalue differences of absolute value less than
one. Lemma~\ref{lem:rigidity} instead gives the required conclusion directly
for every rational invariant subspace of the stated self-adjoint connection,
including when $H$ has repeated eigenvalues.

The degree bound on the spanning family cannot be improved in general.
This concerns the degree needed to generate the cyclic module; the trace
degree needed to distinguish trees is considered in Section~\ref{sec:lower}.

\begin{proposition}[The probe degree is necessary]\label{prop:probedegree}
For every $N\ge1$, there is a symmetric pair satisfying
Theorem~\ref{thm:probes} for which every polynomial family spanning the cyclic
module has a member of degree at least $N-1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $K=0$, $H=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_N)$ with distinct
real $\lambda_i$, and $v=\one$. The Vandermonde matrix shows that
$v,Hv,\ldots,H^{N-1}v$ are independent. Every polynomial of total degree at
most $d$ evaluated at $(H,0)$ sends $v$ into
$\spanop\{v,Hv,\ldots,H^dv\}$, whose dimension is at most $d+1$.
Spanning this $N$-dimensional module therefore requires $d\ge N-1$.
\end{proof}

\section{A single trace invariant for trees}\label{sec:reconstruction}
The fixed spanning family lets us replace all principal moments by three
finite Gram matrices recording the inner product and both generator actions
on the principal module. We first identify these complete data, then give
their trace encoding and its circuit factorization. The proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:tree} verifies the encoding and its resource bounds together.
The graph-theoretic input is the following reconstruction theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Principal-moment reconstruction; {\cite[Theorem 5.4]{LuLi}}]\label{thm:input}
If two trees $T,T'$ satisfy
\[
\one^{\mathsf T}w(A_T,D_T)\one
=\one^{\mathsf T}w(A_{T'},D_{T'})\one
\quad\text{for every word }w\in\{x,y\}^{*},
\]
then $T\cong T'$.
\end{theorem}

To recover every pointed moment, it suffices to recover the inner product,
the seed, and the action of both generators on its cyclic module. The correspondence is
standard; for the all-ones seed, see \cite[Lemma 3.4]{GRS}. Finite word-Gram
reconstruction also underlies the unitary-similarity criteria of Al'pin and
Ikramov \cite[Proposition 10 and Theorem 5]{AlpinIkramov}. The following
pointed real-symmetric form shows that a prescribed spanning family and its
three Gram matrices recover precisely this information.

\begin{lemma}[Recovering a pointed representation]\label{lem:gram}
Let $H,K$ be real symmetric matrices, let $v$ be a vector, and suppose the vectors
$z_i=U_i(H,K)v$, $0\le i<M$, span $\M(H,K;v)$, where $U_0=1$.
The three matrices $G,G^H,G^K$ with entries
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gram}
G_{ij}=z_i^{\mathsf T}z_j,\qquad
G^H_{ij}=z_i^{\mathsf T}Hz_j,\qquad
G^K_{ij}=z_i^{\mathsf T}Kz_j
\end{equation}
determine this pointed representation up to a seed-preserving isometry.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose two pointed pairs give the same three matrices. Define
$\sum_i c_i z_i\mapsto\sum_i c_i z_i'$.
Equality of $G$ makes this map well defined and isometric, and the spanning
hypothesis makes it onto. Equality of $G^H$ implies that the map intertwines
the restrictions of $H$ and $H'$: pair the desired identity with each spanning
vector $z_i'$. Equality of $G^K$ gives the same conclusion for $K,K'$.
Finally, $z_0=v$ and $z_0'=v'$, so the seed is preserved.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Finite invariants for pointed representations]\label{cor:pointed}
For real symmetric pairs of dimension at most $N$ satisfying the hypothesis
of Theorem~\ref{thm:probes}, the matrices in \eqref{eq:gram} formed from its
fixed polynomials are complete invariants of the pointed cyclic representation.
Their entries are polynomial functions of $(H,K,v)$, of degree at most $2N-1$
in the matrix entries and degree two in the seed. Equality of the three matrices
is equivalent to a seed-preserving isometry intertwining both restricted generators.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
One direction is Lemma~\ref{lem:gram}. For the other, an intertwining isometry
preserves every vector $U_i(H,K)v$ and hence all three matrices.
The degree bound follows from $\deg U_i\le N-1$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{From Gram data to a trace expression.}
For trees, the resulting isometry preserves every principal moment, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:input} gives a graph isomorphism. To express each of these
integer matrix entries as a trace, we use McKay's distance polynomials
\cite[Lemma 5.1 and Corollary 5.2]{McKay}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:distance}
\begin{aligned}
P_0&=1,\qquad P_1=x,\qquad P_2=x^2-y,\\
P_k&=xP_{k-1}-(y-1)P_{k-2}\quad(k\ge3),\qquad
J_N=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}P_k.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
All these expressions belong to $\Z\langle x,y\rangle$.
On a tree $T$, let $F_k(u,v)$ be the indicator of $\operatorname{dist}(u,v)=k$.
Then $F_0=I$ and $F_1=A_T$. The diagonal of $A_T^2$ is $D_T$, and two distinct
vertices have one common neighbour exactly when their distance is two; hence
$F_2=A_T^2-D_T$. For $k\ge3$, count neighbours $w$ of $u$ with
$\operatorname{dist}(w,v)=k-1$. The unique path from $u$ to $v$ gives one such
neighbour when $\operatorname{dist}(u,v)=k$, and $\deg(u)-1$ when
$\operatorname{dist}(u,v)=k-2$; otherwise the count is zero. Thus
$A_TF_{k-1}=F_k+(D_T-I)F_{k-2}$, proving $P_k(A_T,D_T)=F_k$ by induction.
Every distance is at most $n-1\le N-1$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J}
J_N(A_T,D_T)=\one\one^{\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}
In particular, $\tr(J_N(A_T,D_T)X)=\one^{\mathsf T}X\one$ for every matrix $X$.
The expression $J_N$ has degree at most $N-1$; we keep its formal adjoint
$J_N^*$ in the construction below.

It remains to encode the entries of $G,G^A,G^D$ in a single trace and to compute
that trace with $O(N^2)$ gates. Enumerate the $M=N(N+1)/2$ polynomials
in \eqref{eq:triangle} in lexicographic order of the index pair, as
$U_0,\ldots,U_{M-1}$, with $U_0=1$. Set
\[
X_\nu=U_i^*SU_j,\qquad S\in\{1,x,y\},\quad 0\le i,j<M,
\]
where $\nu=sM^2+iM+j$ and $s=0,1,2$ corresponds to $S=1,x,y$, respectively.
There are $Q=3M^2$ entries to encode. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radix}
L=3N^3,\qquad R=L^{N-1},\qquad H_N=N^2R^2,\qquad b_N=2H_N+1.
\end{equation}
For $N=1$ take $p_1=1$; there is only one nonempty tree of order at most one.
For $N\ge2$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:final}
p_N=\sum_{\nu=0}^{Q-1}b_N^\nu
             \bigl(J_NX_\nu+X_\nu^*J_N^*\bigr).
\end{equation}
This expression is formally self-adjoint. The bounds in \eqref{eq:radix} will
ensure that its trace uniquely encodes all $Q$ integer entries.

The sum in \eqref{eq:final} has $O(N^4)$ terms. The exponent $sM^2+iM+j$
separates the generator index from the two probe indices, allowing the
circuit to share two probe combinations and one generator combination. Put
\[
\begin{aligned}
Y_N&=\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}b_N^jU_j,&
Z_N&=\sum_{i=0}^{M-1}b_N^{iM}U_i,\\
W_N&=1+b_N^{M^2}x+b_N^{2M^2}y.
\end{aligned}
\]
Expanding the product and using $W_N^*=W_N$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factored}
p_N=J_NZ_N^*W_NY_N+Y_N^*W_NZ_NJ_N^*.
\end{equation}
The coefficient of $J_NU_i^*SU_j$ is exactly $b_N^{sM^2+iM+j}$, as in
\eqref{eq:final}. The circuit evaluates \eqref{eq:factored} directly from
the original-order probe matrices and $J_N$; the Gram entries are recovered
only from the final trace in the correctness argument.

The only large prescribed scalar is $b_N$. Starting from $B=b_NI$, at most
$M$ multiplications construct $B^M=b_N^MI$, and at most another $M$ construct
$(B^M)^M=b_N^{M^2}I$. Squaring the latter gives $b_N^{2M^2}I$.
Horner's rule with bases $B$ and $B^M$ constructs $Y_N$ and $Z_N$ in $O(M)$
further gates. Thus these large scalar powers are computed by the circuit.

\paragraph{Correctness and resource bounds.}
The construction is now fixed. Its correctness rests on the completeness of
the Gram data and the injectivity of their bounded-integer encoding; the
factorization then supplies the circuit bounds.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:tree}]
The case $N=1$ follows from the identity output. Assume $N\ge2$.
For every permitted tree, $A_T,D_T$ are symmetric and
$\Spec(D_T)\subseteq[0,N-1]$. Theorem~\ref{thm:probes} therefore applies.
Lemma~\ref{lem:gram} and Theorem~\ref{thm:input} show that $G,G^A,G^D$ together
form a complete tree invariant.

Their entries are integers. Since the induced row-sum norms of $A_T,D_T$ are
at most $N-1$, induction in \eqref{eq:triangle} gives
\[
\|U_{i,j}(A_T,D_T)\one\|_\infty\le L^i.
\]
The same bound holds for the induced matrix norms of $U_{i,j}$.
Consequently, for $S=I,A_T,D_T$,
\[
|\one^{\mathsf T}U_i^*SU_j\one|\le N^2R^2=H_N.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:J} now gives
\[
\tr p_N(A_T,D_T)=2\sum_{\nu=0}^{Q-1}b_N^\nu h_\nu(T),
\qquad h_\nu(T)=\one^{\mathsf T}X_\nu(A_T,D_T)\one.
\]
This integer encoding is injective for entries in $[-H_N,H_N]$.
Indeed, at the least index where two sequences differ, the nonzero difference
has absolute value at most $2H_N<b_N$ and cannot be divisible by $b_N$.
Equality of final traces is therefore equivalent to equality of all entries
of $G,G^A,G^D$, and hence to tree isomorphism. In particular, $h_0(T)=n$
records the order.

For the gate count, the shared probe circuit uses $O(N^2)$ gates, the distance
recurrence constructs $J_N$ in $O(N)$ gates, and the factored expression
\eqref{eq:factored} uses $O(M)=O(N^2)$ further gates. Reversing multiplication
gates computes formal adjoints at a constant-factor increase in size.
Every operation is prescribed by $N$.

For the degree bound, scalar-power registers have degree zero,
$Y_N,Z_N$ have degree at most $N-1$, and $W_N$ has degree at most one.
Together with $\deg J_N\le N-1$, this gives degree at most $3N-2$.
All scalar coefficients are integers, and \eqref{eq:factored} is self-adjoint.

For the bit bounds, $\log_2 b_N=O(N\log(N+1))$. The reversed-product recurrence
gives the same matrix norm bound $R$ for $U_i^*$. Scalar-power registers have
exponent at most $2M^2$, and the partial Horner sums for $Y_N,Z_N$ have
exponents at most $M-1$ and $M(M-1)$, respectively. On trees,
$\|J_N\|_\infty\le N$, and intermediate products in the distance recurrence
have polynomially bounded entries. Every register is consequently bounded by
polynomial factors in $N$ times $R^2b_N^{3M^2}$. Since $M=O(N^2)$, all stored
integer entries and the final trace have $O(N^5\log(N+1))$ bits.
These bounds also give polynomial time for constructing and evaluating the
circuit in the bit model.
\end{proof}

\section{A linear trace-degree lower bound}\label{sec:lower}
We now prove that the linear degree in Theorem~\ref{thm:tree} is necessary up to a constant factor. The obstruction comes from two spiders whose short degree-weighted closed walks have identical counts. This is a bound on polynomial degree; gate counts and bit complexity are separate resources.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lower}
For $m\ge2$, let $T_m$ and $T'_m$ be the spiders with arm lengths
\[
(m,m,m+2)\quad\text{and}\quad(m,m+1,m+1),
\]
respectively. Both have $3m+3$ vertices and maximum degree three. Every word $w\in\{x,y\}^{*}$ containing fewer than $2m$ occurrences of $x$ satisfies $\tr w(A_{T_m},D_{T_m})=\tr w(A_{T'_m},D_{T'_m})$. Consequently, any polynomial in $\R\langle x,y\rangle$ whose trace separates all trees of order at most $N$ has degree at least $2\lfloor(N-3)/3\rfloor$ for $N\ge9$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A trace word with $s$ adjacency factors is a sum over closed walks of length $s$, with products of vertex degrees attached at prescribed positions. On a tree, such a walk traverses every edge of its image at least twice. Its image therefore has at most $s/2<m$ edges.

Call the center and the three leaves special. The image cannot contain two special vertices. If it contains the center, its degree-labelled embeddings are the same in both spiders, since every arm has length at least $m$. If it contains a leaf but not the center, its image is a short path and the three leaf neighbourhoods agree. If it contains no special vertex, it is a path of length $\ell<m$ inside the degree-two portion of an arm. An arm of length $L_a$ has $L_a-1-\ell$ such undirected placements, so their total is $\sum_a L_a-3(\ell+1)$, equal for the two spiders. Orientations, choices of starting position, and repeated-walk patterns contribute the same multiplicities to each placement. Hence every degree-labelled walk count, and therefore every trace word in question, agrees. The case $s=0$ follows directly from the common degree multiset.

A polynomial of total degree less than $2m$ is a linear combination of such words. The unique degree-three center has two leaves at distance $m$ in $T_m$ and one in $T'_m$, so the spiders are nonisomorphic. Taking $m=\lfloor(N-3)/3\rfloor$ proves the stated bound.
\end{proof}

Together with Theorem~\ref{thm:tree}, this gives degree $\Theta(N)$ for a single-trace separator. The constants in the upper and lower bounds need not agree.

\section{A fixed cyclic representative}\label{sec:cyclic}
We give a further construction from the fixed spanning family: a polynomial $C_N\in\Z\langle x,y\rangle$ that is a sum of Hermitian squares and whose value on each tree has image $\M_T$ and is cyclic from $\one$ on that module. This construction is independent of the trace encoding in Theorem~\ref{thm:tree}. In vector formulas in this appendix, polynomial expressions are evaluated at $(A_T,D_T)$.

\subsection{Fixed rank condensation}
The probes span $\M_T$, but a fixed initial segment need not be a basis.
We need fixed linear combinations whose first $r$ responses are independent
whenever $r=\dim\M_T$. This prefix property will make the orthogonal residuals
vanish exactly after the rank, without choosing pivots from the input.

Assume $N\ge2$. Let $U_0=1,U_1,\ldots,U_{M-1}$ be the probes. With $L,R$ as in \eqref{eq:radix}, set
\[
H_0=(M-1)R\,2^{(N-2)(M-2)},\qquad
H_1=N!H_0^{N-1},\qquad \tau=H_1+2.
\]
Define $Z_0=1$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:folded}
Z_{j+1}=\sum_{i=1}^{M-1}(2^j\tau)^{i-1}U_i,
\qquad 0\le j\le N-2.
\end{equation}
These sums again use Horner's rule. Their degree is at most $N-1$, their shared size is polynomial, and their coefficients and evaluated entries have polynomial bit length. More explicitly, $\log\tau=O(N^4)$ and the evaluated vector entries have $O(N^6)$ bits.

\begin{lemma}[A basis in the first positions]\label{lem:prefix}
On every tree of order at most $N$, if $r=\dim\M_T$, then
\[
z_0=\one,\ z_1=Z_1\one,\ldots,z_{r-1}=Z_{r-1}\one
\]
are independent. All $N$ vectors $z_i$ belong to $\M_T$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $r=1$, the assertion follows from $z_0=\one\ne0$. Assume $r\ge2$. Work first with a commuting variable $t$ in place of $\tau$, and pass to the quotient of $\M_T$ by $\R\one$. The coefficient vectors of
\[
F(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{M-1}U_i\one\,t^{i-1}
\]
span this quotient. Choose linear coordinates on the quotient, and by constant row operations put the resulting $r-1$ independent coordinate polynomials in a basis of distinct degrees $d_1,\ldots,d_{r-1}$. The leading coefficient of the determinant of their evaluations at
\[
t,2t,\ldots,2^{r-2}t
\]
is the product of their nonzero leading coefficients times
\[
\det\bigl[(2^{d_i})^j\bigr]_{1\le i\le r-1,\,0\le j\le r-2}\ne0.
\]
Independence in the quotient gives full column rank over $\R(t)$ in the original vertex coordinates. Thus some $r\times r$ coordinate minor of
$[\one,F(t),F(2t),\ldots,F(2^{r-2}t)]$ is a nonzero integer polynomial.

Each polynomial entry outside the first column has coefficient $\ell_1$ norm at most $H_0$. Every such minor has coefficient $\ell_1$ norm at most $N!H_0^{N-1}=H_1$. A nonzero integer polynomial with this bound has no real root greater than $H_1+1$: its leading coefficient has absolute value at least one and dominates the lower coefficients there. Therefore the same minor is nonzero at $t=\tau$.
\end{proof}

The evaluations $f(t)\mapsto(f(t),f(2t),\ldots)$ use the folded-Wronskian
rank-preservation construction of Forbes and Shpilka (full version,
\cite[Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.3]{FS12}), building on the method of Gabizon
and Raz (earlier ECCC version, \cite[Lemma 6.1]{GabizonRaz}). See also its use in subspace designs
\cite{GK} and the full version of \cite[Construction 2.7]{FG}.
This construction gives a nonzero minor
polynomial. The coefficient bound above then supplies a single integer
specialization $\tau$ valid for all the bounded integer inputs considered here.

\subsection{Fraction-free orthogonal residuals}
The prefix property permits one orthogonalization formula for every rank.
Multiplying each residual by the preceding Gram determinant removes division
and keeps the expression polynomial.
For $0\le k<N$, let $K_k=[z_0\ \cdots\ z_{k-1}]$ and $G_k=K_k^{\mathsf T}K_k$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual}
u_0=\one,\qquad
u_k=(\det G_k)z_k-K_k\adj(G_k)K_k^{\mathsf T}z_k\quad(k\ge1).
\end{equation}
For $k<r$, $u_k$ is a nonzero multiple of the orthogonal residual of $z_k$ against its predecessors. Hence $u_0,\ldots,u_{r-1}$ are mutually orthogonal and nonzero. For $k=r<N$, the matrix $G_r$ is invertible and $z_r\in\im K_r$, so $u_r=0$. For $k>r$ they also vanish: then $\det G_k=0$ and
\[
K_k\adj(G_k)=0.
\]
Indeed $G_k\adj(G_k)=0$, and $\ker(K_k^{\mathsf T}K_k)=\ker K_k$.

To make the first vector cyclic, we couple consecutive residuals through
outer products of their adjacent sums.

Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jacobi}
C^{\mathrm{aux}}=u_0u_0^{\mathsf T}
       +\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}(u_k+u_{k+1})(u_k+u_{k+1})^{\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}
In the orthonormal basis $u_i/\|u_i\|$, its restriction to $\M_T$ is tridiagonal with strictly positive off-diagonal entries. Powers of this matrix applied to the first basis vector successively have nonzero coordinates in positions $1,2,\ldots,r$. Thus it is cyclic from $u_0=\one$. Moreover, $u_0$ together with the adjacent sums $u_k+u_{k+1}$ spans $\M_T$. The kernel of their positive-semidefinite outer-product sum is exactly $\M_T^\perp$, so its image is $\M_T$.

The vector construction gives the required image and cyclicity. To realize
these properties by a polynomial in $x,y$, we must express the Gram
determinants using matrices of the original input order. On a tree of order $n$,
multiples of $J_N$ carry the scalar expressions through $J_N^2=nJ_N$; the resulting positive
rescalings of the residuals preserve both properties. This yields the
following circuit construction.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform cyclic principal matrix]\label{thm:uniformcyclic}
There is a deterministic polynomial-time construction, from $N$ alone, of a polynomial $C_N\in\Z\langle x,y\rangle$, represented by an integer matrix circuit and formally a sum of Hermitian squares, with degree at most $2(4N-3)(N-1)$ for $N\ge2$ and polynomial size and bit complexity such that, on every tree of order at most $N$,
\[
C_N\succeq0,\qquad \im C_N=\M_T,
\qquad \spanop\{\one,C_N\one,\ldots,C_N^{N-1}\one\}=\M_T.
\]
All its gates act on the original matrix space. Formula \eqref{eq:residual} requires no determinant, inverse, rank, or trace oracle.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $N=1$ return $C_1=I$. Assume $N\ge2$, and abbreviate $J_N$ to $J$. Compute original-order matrix registers
\[
B_{ij}=JZ_i^*Z_jJ.
\]
On a tree of order $n$, these satisfy $B_{ij}=g_{ij}J$, where $g_{ij}=z_i^{\mathsf T}z_j$, and $J^2=nJ$. A homogeneous scalar polynomial $f$ of positive degree $q$ in the Gram entries can therefore be compiled so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:homogeneous-lift}
\widehat f(B)=n^{q-1}f(g)J.
\end{equation}
Indeed, addition of equal degrees preserves this identity, while multiplication of positive degrees $p,q$ gives the factor $n^{p+q-1}$ by $J^2=nJ$. Degree-zero constants act by prescribed integer scaling; they are never represented by a putative multiplicative identity $J$.

Division-free determinant circuits are classical \cite{Berkowitz,Soltys,Bird}. We use the homogeneous form of Bird's recurrence \cite{Bird}, also stated in \cite[Theorem 1.1]{Przezdziecki}: for a $k\times k$ scalar matrix $G$, put $F_1=G$ and $F_{q+1}=\mu(F_q)G$, where $\mu(F)$ keeps the strictly upper-triangular entries, has zero entries below the diagonal, and has diagonal entries $-\sum_{\ell>i}F_{\ell\ell}$. Then $(-1)^{k-1}(F_k)_{00}=\det G$. Each stage has homogeneous degree $q$; shared ordinary matrix multiplication implements the scalar recurrence with $O(k^4)$ arithmetic gates. Thus \eqref{eq:homogeneous-lift} applies directly, with all integer coefficients implemented as scaling gates. The auxiliary $k\times k$ arrays index original-order matrix registers. Let $b_k=(g_{ik})_{i<k}$ and let $G_k^{(i)}$ be $G_k$ with column $i$ replaced by $b_k$. The degree-$k$ determinant circuits give
\[
\widehat\Delta_k=n^{k-1}\det(G_k)J,\qquad
\widehat\Delta_{k,i}=n^{k-1}\det(G_k^{(i)})J.
\]
Define $V_0=J$ and, for $1\le k<N$,
\[
V_k=Z_k\widehat\Delta_k-\sum_{i<k}Z_i\widehat\Delta_{k,i}.
\]
The polynomial column-replacement identity
$\det(G_k^{(i)})=(\adj(G_k)b_k)_i$ holds also when $G_k$ is singular. Thus on trees $V_k=n^{k-1}u_k\one^{\mathsf T}$. With $t_0=\one$ and $t_k=n^{k-1}u_k$ for $k\ge1$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:integer-sos}
C_N=V_0V_0^*+\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}(V_k+V_{k+1})(V_k+V_{k+1})^*.
\end{equation}
Its value is $n[t_0t_0^{\mathsf T}+\sum_k(t_k+t_{k+1})(t_k+t_{k+1})^{\mathsf T}]$. The $t_i$ have the same orthogonal nonzero prefix and zero tail as the $u_i$. The Jacobi and image arguments following \eqref{eq:jacobi} apply with these positive rescalings, proving the three conclusions. Formula \eqref{eq:integer-sos} is formally self-adjoint and evaluates to a positive semidefinite matrix on every real symmetric pair; the image and cyclicity claims use the spanning and independence properties proved above.

Since $\deg Z_i,\deg J\le N-1$, we have
\[
\deg B_{ij}\le4(N-1),\quad
\deg\widehat\Delta_k,\deg\widehat\Delta_{k,i}\le4k(N-1),
\]
and $\deg V_k\le(4k+1)(N-1)$. Equation~\eqref{eq:integer-sos} consequently has degree at most $2(4N-3)(N-1)$. There are polynomially many determinant circuits of order at most $N$. Their auxiliary arrays consist of original-order matrix registers.

For the bit bound, let $c_q$ be the largest coefficient $\ell_1$ norm of an
entry of $F_q$, viewed as a polynomial in the scalar matrix entries.
The stated Bird recurrence gives
\[
c_1=1,\qquad c_{q+1}\le k^2c_q,\qquad c_q\le k^{2(q-1)}.
\]
Indeed, an entry of $\mu(F_q)$ is an entry of $F_q$ or a signed sum of at
most $k$ such entries, and matrix multiplication sums at most $k$ products.
If the scalar inputs have absolute value at most $A\ge1$, then
$|(F_q)_{ij}|\le k^{2(q-1)}A^q$, with the same type of bound for partial
sums in each stage. Thus their bit lengths are
$O(q(\log(k+1)+\log(A+1)))$.
Here the Gram entries have $O(N^6)$ bits and $q\le k\le N$.
The homogeneous lift adds powers of $n$ of exponent at most $N-1$;
the subsequent products and sums in \eqref{eq:integer-sos} preserve a
polynomial bound on every register's bit length.
\end{proof}

\section{A full-representation route using only McKay}\label{sec:fulltrace}
There is also a polynomial-resource construction using McKay's full adjacency--degree reconstruction theorem as its graph-theoretic input. It has degree $O(N^2)$ and $O(N^4)$ gates. The written argument below uses the classical reconstruction theorem directly; Appendix~\ref{sec:verification} specifies the different reconstruction dependency of the formal development.

To invoke McKay's theorem directly, we recover all word traces. These are
pointed moments for left multiplication on the Frobenius space with seed $I$,
so the same finite Gram reconstruction applies with dimension bound $N^2$.
Fix a tree $T$ of order $n\le N$ and write $A=A_T$, $D=D_T$. Equip $M_n(\R)$ with the Frobenius inner product and seed $I$. Left multiplication by $A$ and $D$ gives symmetric operators in dimension $n^2\le N^2$; the latter has spectral diameter at most $N-1$. Theorem~\ref{thm:probes} with dimension bound $N^2$, and hence recurrence scale $N^4$, gives $O(N^4)$ polynomials of degree at most $N^2-1$ spanning $\langle A,D\rangle$.

The three matrices of Frobenius inner products, with entries
\[
\tr(U_i^*U_j),\quad\tr(U_i^*AU_j),\quad\tr(U_i^*DU_j)
\]
determine the pointed left-regular representation with seed $I$. Their $(0,0)$ Gram entry is $\tr I=n$, so equality first gives the same order. It also implies equality of $\tr w(A,D)$ for every word $w$. For every fixed matrix polynomial $q$, equality also holds for $\tr(q^k)$ for every $k$. Newton identities therefore give the same characteristic polynomial for $q$ on the two inputs. McKay's theorem \cite[Theorem 5.3]{McKay} then distinguishes trees.

These Gram entries are already traces, so their integer encoding requires
no additional factor $J_N$. The factoring used in \eqref{eq:factored}
applies directly. If $M'=O(N^4)$ is the number of probes and $b$ exceeds twice a uniform bound on their integer entries, put $Y=\sum_jb^jU_j$, $Z=\sum_ib^{iM'}U_i$, and $W=1+b^{M'^2}x+b^{2M'^2}y$. Then
\[
p_N^{\mathrm{full}}=Z^*WY+Y^*WZ
\]
encodes all entries of the three matrices using $O(M')=O(N^4)$ gates and degree at most $2N^2-1$. All evaluations still use matrices of the original order; the Frobenius-space enlargement is only in the spanning proof. The resulting polynomial belongs to $\Z\langle x,y\rangle$. The recurrence and integer encoding give polynomial bit complexity by the same norm estimates as in Section~\ref{sec:reconstruction}, with the larger dimension bound. The output expression uses only the polynomials spanning the full algebra and their formal adjoints.

\section{Several symmetric generators}\label{sec:multi}
We give the direct extension of Theorem~\ref{thm:probes}. Here
$\Z\langle x_0,\ldots,x_s\rangle$ denotes the free associative algebra
in $s+1$ noncommuting variables, and $t$ commutes with every generator.
For $s,N\ge1$, set
\[
\kappa=sN^2,\qquad a_i=i-1,\qquad
D(t)=\prod_{i=1}^s(t-a_i),\qquad Q_i(t)=\prod_{j\ne i}(t-a_j).
\]
Define $F_k\in\Z\langle x_0,\ldots,x_s\rangle[t]$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multi-recurrence}
\begin{aligned}
F_0&=1,\\
F_{k+1}&=\kappa D F_k'
 +\left(\kappa D x_0+\sum_{i=1}^sQ_ix_i-k\kappa D'\right)F_k.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Write $F_k=\sum_{j=0}^{sk}P_{k,j}t^j$, including zero coefficients.

\begin{corollary}[Fixed probes for several symmetric generators]\label{cor:multi}
Let $s,N\ge1$ and $1\le n\le N$. Suppose that
$H,A_1,\ldots,A_s\in M_n(\R)$ are symmetric and
$\diam\Spec(A_i)\le N-1$ for every $i$. For every $v\in\R^n$, let
\[
\M=\spanop_\R\{w(H,A_1,\ldots,A_s)v:
                 w\in\{x_0,\ldots,x_s\}^*\},\qquad r=\dim\M.
\]
Then
\[
\spanop_\R\{P_{k,j}(H,A_1,\ldots,A_s)v:0\le k<r,\ 0\le j\le sk\}
=\M.
\]
In particular, the full family with $k<N$ spans $\M$ and has
$N+sN(N-1)/2$ indexed members. It is constructed from $(s,N)$ alone,
has noncommutative degree at most $N-1$, and admits a shared division-free
circuit of $O(s^2N^2)$ gates on matrices of the original order.
Its prescribed integer constants have
$O(s\log(s+1)+\log(N+1))$ bits. No spectral bound is required for $H$.
For $r=0$, the prefix is the empty family.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
We first extend Lemma~\ref{lem:rigidity}. Let the $a_i$ be distinct real
numbers and let $H,B_1,\ldots,B_s$ be symmetric. If a rational subspace
$V$ of rank $r$ is invariant under
\[
\nabla=\partial_t+H+\sum_{i=1}^s\frac{B_i}{t-a_i},
\qquad r\sum_{i=1}^s\diam\Spec(B_i)<1,
\]
then it is the scalar extension of a real subspace invariant separately
under $H,B_1,\ldots,B_s$. The rank-zero case is immediate. Otherwise choose
a primitive polynomial exterior vector $w(t)$ representing $V$, of degree
$\ell$ with nonzero leading coefficient $w_\ell$. The induced connection
satisfies
\[
w'+\left(H^{[r]}+\sum_i\frac{B_i^{[r]}}{t-a_i}\right)w=c(t)w.
\]
A B\'ezout combination of the coordinates shows that $Dc$ is polynomial;
degree comparison gives $\deg(Dc)\le s$. Consequently
$c=\beta+\sum_i\alpha_i/(t-a_i)$. Primitivity gives $w(a_i)\ne0$ at every
pole, and taking residues yields
$B_i^{[r]}w(a_i)=\alpha_iw(a_i)$.

Suppose $\ell>0$. Comparing the two highest terms at infinity gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
H^{[r]}w_\ell&=\beta w_\ell,\\
\ell w_\ell+H^{[r]}w_{\ell-1}+\sum_iB_i^{[r]}w_\ell
 &=\beta w_{\ell-1}+\left(\sum_i\alpha_i\right)w_\ell.
\end{aligned}
\]
Equivalently, these are the highest two coefficient identities after
multiplication by $D$, with its next-to-leading coefficient cancelled
using the first identity. Taking the inner product of the second identity
with $w_\ell$ cancels the $H^{[r]}$ terms and gives
\[
\ell=\sum_i\left(\alpha_i-
 \frac{\langle w_\ell,B_i^{[r]}w_\ell\rangle}{\|w_\ell\|^2}\right)
\le\sum_i\diam\Spec(B_i^{[r]})
\le r\sum_i\diam\Spec(B_i)<1.
\]
Thus $\ell=0$. The constant exterior line defines a real subspace $W$
with $V=W\otimes_\R\R(t)$. Projecting $\nabla u$ to $\R^n/W$ for $u\in W$
and comparing its residues and constant part proves separate invariance.

Apply this result with $B_i=A_i/\kappa$. For every $r\le N$,
\[
r\sum_i\diam\Spec(B_i)\le\frac{N-1}{N}<1.
\]
Let $q_0=v$ and $q_{k+1}=\nabla q_k$. As in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:probes}, the first dependence is stable by the Leibniz
rule. Its rational span lies in $\M\otimes_\R\R(t)$; rigidity makes it
constant and separately invariant, and it contains $v$. Minimality of
$\M$ therefore identifies this span with $\M\otimes_\R\R(t)$.
The first $r$ iterates span it. Direct differentiation shows that
$f_k=\kappa^kD^kq_k$ obeys \eqref{eq:multi-recurrence} evaluated on $v$.
Induction gives $\deg_t F_k\le sk$ and $\deg P_{k,j}\le k$.
Every coefficient response lies in $\M$. Conversely, the first $r$
iterates belong to the scalar extension of the span of those coefficients;
faithfulness of scalar extension proves the asserted real spanning equality.
The full indexed family contains
$\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(sk+1)=N+sN(N-1)/2$ coefficients.

For the circuit bound, put $G=DF_k$ and $T=DF_k'$. Multiplication by $D$
can be performed successively through its $s$ monic linear factors.
From the shared $G$, synthetic division by $t-a_i$ computes
$Q_iF_k$ using only additions and multiplication by the fixed integer
$a_i$. Since $D'F_k=G'-T$, the next row can be computed as
\[
F_{k+1}=\kappa(k+1)T+\kappa x_0G
             +\sum_i x_i(Q_iF_k)-k\kappa G'.
\]
Scalar polynomials commute with the noncommuting generators, so this is
exactly \eqref{eq:multi-recurrence}. The factor products and all $s$
synthetic divisions require $O(s^2(k+1))$ gates in row $k$; the derivative
and generator operations obey the same bound. Summing over $k<N$ gives
$O(s^2N^2)$. This factor implementation needs only pole values, derivative
indices and integer multiples of $\kappa$, which satisfy the stated
constant-bit bound. All generator multiplications are on the left and
all matrix registers retain the input order. For $s=1$, $D=t$ and
\eqref{eq:multi-recurrence} gives exactly \eqref{eq:triangle}, with
$x_0=x$ and $x_1=y$.
\end{proof}

The bound $N-1$ remains necessary: set every $A_i=0$ and use the diagonal
$H$ and all-ones seed from Proposition~\ref{prop:probedegree}.
The case of a single generator ($s=0$) is the ordinary Krylov family
$1,H,\ldots,H^{N-1}$ and is separate from the formula $\kappa=sN^2$.
